We went to Croatia last month, and because I thought it would be funny, I spent the whole flight watching TED Talks. Okay not the whole flight (how psycho would that have been), but I did make it through like... 5-6. Just for the bit. Which no one thought was funny but me. But that's worth it, right?  

Here are the top three: 
- [12 truths I learned from life and writing](https://www.ted.com/talks/anne_lamott_12_truths_i_learned_from_life_and_writing) by Anne Lamott
- [What happened when I started measuring my life every day](https://www.ted.com/talks/chris_musser_what_happened_when_i_started_measuring_my_life_every_day) by Chris Musser
- [The Art of Reading Minds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h3M00JI8Iwo) by Oz Pearlman

Oz Pearlman's was... insane. I don't know much [about him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oz_Pearlman), but his TED talk made it seem like he could legitimately read ppl's minds. Slightly upsettingly, and  gave me the feeling that there was some other trickery going on. 

Musser had a life tracker not so dissimilar from mine! So I was curious. He kind of bombed, and even watching on a plane I wanted to distance myself from him by silently mouthing to my plane neighbors *I don't really like this guy fyi, I'm barely paying attention*. 

His talk was interesting, though, and it did stick with me. Essentially, he fills out a [survey](https://goodlifetracker.com/) every day of how well he's doing across a couple of categories. He has a way to see trends over weeks/months etc, and that lets him discover quickly what area in his life is going downhill fast. 

Lamott's was my fav. Funny, heartfelt, and full of insight and one-liners, like, "Grace always bats last."
