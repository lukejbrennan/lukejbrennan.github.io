Day 5. Lemonade stand. Electric.

Yeahhhhh I went to a lemonade stand for Day 5. That's how freaking ~IN~summer I am. 
It's the height of summer baby, and I'm going to a lemonade stand.

Catch me living up fall when I’m sipping a coffee at colectivo, crying, reading *Tomorrow, Tomorrow, Tomorrow*, with a latte, by the fire, with leaves literally FALLING around me, like in Aragorn's memory in Lord of the Rings. That’ll be me in fall. 

In winter? Snuggled. Surrounded by candles. And fuzzy blankets. Watching elf while making Christmas cookies. On the couch. Fire blazing. 

In springtime I’m gonna be sipping on the chilliest beer. First day above 40 degrees, I'm going to be out on a patio. But it’s summertime baby, and there’s a lemonade stand, and I’m hitting it up!

If you haven't been around, I've been giving out lil books about ppl pooping their pants. Sort of a collective memoir kind of thing. It's short. Punchy. 

Anyway. I didn't give one out today. 

Instead, I gave the money that I put *inside* the book (because - yeah that was a thing from Day 4, I'm actually paying ppl to *take* my book from me. And sometimes they still say no!)

Yeah I'll take that one outside of the parenthesis, actually. Let's -- can we get this one up on the big screen? Captions, yeah. Put the captions on.

I just want to generate some positive karma, okay? I-store-my-next-meal-in-the-stomach-of-my-brother energy. Braiding sweet grass! Gifts of the Serviceberry!

It's weird when a stranger tries to talk to you! And the title (*You’re Going to Shit Yourself*) could be seen as…aggressive. So I felt like I wanted to put a little sentiment in there (sometimes a note, sometimes money, sometimes both) to get the juicies flowing a little bit. Loosen people up.

But good god if that's not the most pick-me energy I've ever put on. To ask this guy: "Do you want my book??" And have him say no, lmfao. 

But anyway, back to the lemonade stand. For some reason, I felt like I had to approach them like a business, like a REAL business. Like I had to let them know, that I knew, that they were doing real... transactions here. This is real money! They're providing value, and I'm paying for it. 

I didn't play soft, is what I'm saying. I didn't go in without... making sure I knew I was going to get a good deal. 

I didn't ask: "Are y'all selling lemonade?"

No - I could figure that out myself, thanks. 

I went straight to the deal -- How *much* is lemonade? And I said it very uptight. Like if the price was too high, I might just say no. 

I might just walk away.

One sister said: "Yes" and then her sister immediately said: "What'd you mean, 'yes'"

I immediately felt terrible. What was I doing, driving such a hard deal for this single stand farmers market, attended by children. 

The eldest said the lemonade would be .75 cents please, to which I responded, "Easy" which.... in the moment, I'd meant to be a sort of dampener, something to sooth the rising tensions. To say, in a british/australian[^1] accent: "Sorry, I didn't mean to come across rude, or so suspicious of your lemonade prices. Just want you to know -- I'm *definitely* going to be buying some lemonade. There is no world, in which I would have asked the price, determined: not worth it, and walked away. We're on the same team here, and I appreciate your contributions."

But now that I think about it, saying "easy" sort of implies "easy money," like...
